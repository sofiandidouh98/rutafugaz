It is the question you ask us most: **motorhome, campervan or caravan?** The honest answer is that there is no perfect vehicle, only the perfect vehicle for the way you travel.

A couple escaping to the mountains every weekend does not need the same thing as a family with three kids spending a month on the coast, or a digital nomad working from the road ten months a year.

In this comparison we look at the main types with approximate figures for price, driving, fuel use, comfort and resale value, so you can make the decision with your head (and not just with Instagram).

---

## 1. The Vehicle Types, Explained

### Campervan
A van converted into living space. It can be **compact** (like the VW California or Ford Transit Custom Nugget) or a **large panel van** (built on bases such as the Fiat Ducato, Citroën Jumper or Peugeot Boxer, from 5.4 to 6.4 metres long). It keeps the van's original bodywork.

### Motorhome
A living area built onto a cab-chassis. There are three formats:
* **Low-profile (semi-integrated):** The habitation unit joins the cab with an aerodynamic profile. It is the best-selling format: a good balance between space and manoeuvrability.
* **Overcab (alcove):** It has a bed above the cab (the "Luton" or alcove). It is the family favourite thanks to its number of sleeping berths.
* **A-class (integrated):** The original cab disappears and the living area is a single piece. Maximum comfort, a large panoramic windscreen and the highest price.

### Caravan
A habitable trailer you tow with your car. When you reach your destination you unhitch it and still have the car free to get around.

## 3. Driving, Parking and Licence

**The campervan wins hands down for everyday use.** A 5.4 m campervan fits in a slightly larger parking space, gets into many open-air car parks and drives almost like a delivery van. A 6.4 m one already requires more planning.

**Motorhomes** of 7 metres or more are comfortable on the road, but historic centres, villages with narrow streets and car parks with height barriers become a problem.

About the licence (rules common to the whole EU):
* **B licence:** Vehicles up to **3,500 kg MAM**. It covers almost all campervans and most low-profile and overcab motorhomes.
* **C1 licence:** For vehicles from **3,500 to 7,500 kg**. Some large A-class motorhomes require it.
* **Caravan:** With a B licence you can tow a trailer of up to 750 kg, or a heavier one if **the combined MAM of car and caravan does not exceed 3,500 kg**. Above that you need **B96** (combination up to 4,250 kg, with a specific training course) or **BE** (trailer up to 3,500 kg).

## 4. Tolls, Ferries and Hidden Costs

### Tolls
In Spain, a two-axle campervan or motorhome without twin rear wheels usually pays as a light vehicle, although it depends on each toll operator and its criteria (height, axles, wheels). A **car with a caravan** usually pays a higher rate because of the trailer. In France, vehicle height is decisive and many motorhomes pay a more expensive category than a car. **Always check each motorway's rates.**

### Ferries
Ferry companies charge by **length and height**. An 11-12 metre car + caravan combination can cost considerably more than a 6-metre campervan. If you dream of the Balearics, the Canaries or Morocco, this matters.

### Other costs
* **Insurance:** Campervans and motorhomes need their own policy; caravans need their specific one too.
* **Roadworthiness test (ITV in Spain):** All of them undergo periodic inspections, with frequency depending on age and vehicle type.
* **Storage:** If you have no space at home, storing a motorhome or caravan can be a significant monthly cost. A compact campervan sometimes fits in a normal garage.
* **Maintenance:** Motorhomes and caravans have more potential water-ingress points in the habitation unit; it is worth checking for damp regularly.

## 5. Comfort and Space: Where Life Is Better

If you are going to spend weeks inside, comfort rules:

* **Bathroom:** In large panel-van campervans it is usually compact or non-existent; in motorhomes and caravans it is a room of its own, with a separate shower in many models.
* **Interior height:** A high-roof campervan or a motorhome lets you stand up comfortably.
* **Fixed beds:** Motorhomes and caravans offer fixed beds you do not need to make up every night. In a compact campervan, the sofa usually turns into the bed.
* **Rear garage:** Low-profile and overcab models usually have a garage for bikes, chairs and table. In a campervan, storage is the big sacrifice.

## 6. Who Is Each One For?

### Couples
**Large panel-van campervan or small low-profile motorhome.** Agile enough to go anywhere, with enough space for two.

### Families with children
**Overcab or low-profile with bunk beds.** More seats homologated for travelling and sleeping without making up beds. If you are heading to a single destination, a **caravan** is a cheaper alternative.

### Digital nomads
**Large panel-van campervan or low-profile**, with a good electrical setup, a work table and connectivity. If you work on a laptop, electrical autonomy is key; we explain it in our [portable power stations comparison](/en/blog/portable-power-stations-ecoflow-vs-bluetti-campervan).

### Weekend getaways
**Compact campervan.** You can use it daily as a car, park it at home and leave on Friday afternoon without any preparation. On a tight budget, even a [small van converted for under €500](/en/blog/small-van-conversion-under-500-euros) can be the first step.

### Long trips and retirees
**A-class or large low-profile.** The comfort of a home on wheels for stays lasting months.

## 7. Quick Decision Guide

Answer these questions:

1. **How many people will sleep inside?** More than 4 → overcab or caravan.
2. **Will you move every day?** Yes → campervan or motorhome. No (single destination) → caravan.
3. **Do you need it as an everyday vehicle?** Yes → compact campervan.
4. **Do you have somewhere to keep it?** No → a campervan that fits in a garage or on the street.
5. **How much do you want to spend?** Tight → used caravan or used campervan. Comfortable → low-profile or A-class.
6. **Are a full shower and bathroom essential?** Yes → motorhome or caravan.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Which holds its value best?**
Campervans hold their value very well thanks to high demand. Motorhomes do too, although with more variation by brand and condition.

**Can I sleep in a caravan on the street?**
Free overnight stays are intended for self-propelled vehicles such as campervans and motorhomes; caravans are geared towards campsites. Check the local rules of each municipality.

**Does a campervan use less fuel than a motorhome?**
Generally yes, thanks to its lower weight and better aerodynamics, although it depends on the engine and how you drive.

## Our Verdict

* **Choose a campervan** if you prioritise freedom, easy driving, discretion and good resale value.
* **Choose a motorhome** if there are more than two of you or you want the comfort of a home with a full bathroom.
* **Choose a caravan** if you usually travel to one destination, already have a suitable car and want to spend less.

There is no universal answer, but there is a right one for you. Try, compare and choose calmly: the best vehicle is the one that gets you out more often.
