One of the biggest fears when you start travelling in a campervan or motorhome is that sudden sound at midnight: **knocks on the bodywork and torchlight from the local police or the Guardia Civil**.

Misinformation on social media and forums is huge: some claim that *"in Spain everything is forbidden"* and others say that *"inside your vehicle you can do whatever you like because it is an inviolable home"*. Neither extreme is right.

In this guide we break down the regulations actually in force, the well-known **DGT Instruction 08/V-74** (and its PROT 2023/14 update), regional powers and the exact checklist to sleep peacefully anywhere in the country.

---

## 1. The Big Difference: What Is Parking and What Is Camping?

At national level in Spain, the legal distinction between being properly parked and being camped on a public road is drawn by traffic rules (Ministry of the Interior) on one side, and by the tourism and environmental rules of each Autonomous Community on the other.

### When you are PARKED (Completely Legal):
A motorhome or campervan is parked, and not camped, when it meets all of the following conditions at the same time:
1. **It rests only on its own tyres:** You have not lowered hydraulic or mechanical stabiliser legs (only safety chocks are allowed if the vehicle is parked on a steep slope, according to the General Traffic Regulations).
2. **Nothing sticks out beyond its outline:** You have not opened an awning, outward-opening hinged windows (sliding windows are allowed), an awning tent or deployed retractable steps.
3. **You make no disturbing noise and release no fluids outside:** No petrol generator running outside, no grey or black water emptied onto the road and no tables, chairs or barbecues set up on the tarmac.

> **The DGT's golden rule:** As long as the vehicle is properly parked in accordance with the traffic code, **what happens inside it is irrelevant**, whether you are eating, reading or sleeping.

## 2. What You CAN and CANNOT Do on a Public Road

| Action inside the van | Allowed? | Legal detail |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Sleeping, cooking or eating inside** | **YES** | It is private use of the living space. |
| **Opening roof vents (without exceeding the height)** | **YES** | Regulatory upward ventilation. |
| **Opening sliding windows that do not protrude** | **YES** | Does not exceed the vehicle's approved width. |
| **Raising a pop-up roof** | ⚠️ **GREY AREA** | Legally it does not go beyond the horizontal outline, but many regional officers see it as a sign of camping. Avoid it on the coast or in nature parks. |
| **Putting a table, chairs or a stall outside** | ❌ **NO** | It immediately counts as camping on a public road. |
| **Lowering stabiliser legs** | ❌ **NO** | Considered a fixed installation, not parking. |
| **Emptying any kind of water onto the road** | ❌ **NO** | Serious environmental offence. |

## 3. The Traps: Protected Natural Areas and the Coastal Law

There is a hierarchy of rules: environmental laws take precedence over the traffic code. This means that even if you are perfectly parked without taking out a single plate:

1. **National Parks and Nature Parks (PRUG):** They have their own management and use plans. In the vast majority, sleeping in vehicles is strictly forbidden from sunset to sunrise. Fines here range from **€60 to more than €1,000**.
2. **Coastal Law (Maritime-Terrestrial Public Domain):** It prohibits parking and driving vehicles on beaches and their protection easement strips (usually the first 100 to 500 metres from the shoreline). It is one of the most common causes of fines in Andalusia, Galicia and Asturias.

## 4. What to Do If the Police or Forest Rangers Knock on Your Door?

Staying calm and polite is 90% of success. If you find yourself being inspected:

1. **Switch on a soft interior light and open the window politely.** Greeting them calmly immediately defuses the initial tension.
2. **Show your documents in order:** Vehicle registration, valid MOT (ITV) and vehicle insurance.
3. **Argue with the rules in hand:** Politely explain that you are taking a rest stop for road safety reasons (driver fatigue is penalised) and that the vehicle does not exceed its outline or release any fluids.
4. **If you are ordered to move:** Even if you think you are right, never confront or disobey a direct order from an officer. Note down the badge or patrol number, move peacefully and, if appropriate, appeal the fine through administrative channels afterwards.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions About Overnight Parking in a Camper

### Can I be fined for sleeping on a street in a town or city?
If the van is properly parked where any car could park (without blocking driveways, corners or yellow lines) and you do not set anything up outside, **it is completely legal**. Only a specific municipal bylaw (and many of them have been overturned by the courts for contradicting national DGT rules) could restrict it.

### Is it worth using levelling chocks if the street is on a slope?
The General Traffic Regulations allow chocks if the vehicle's weight or the slope require them to prevent it from rolling. However, in crowded tourist areas some officers fine the use of chocks, interpreting it as an intention to camp. If the slope is not extreme, it is wiser to look for another flat spot.
