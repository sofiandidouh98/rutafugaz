Watching your dog race along the shoreline, chase the waves and roll in the sand is one of those moments that make any trip worthwhile. The problem is that, in Spain, **most beaches ban dogs during the bathing season**, and finding one where they are welcome can turn into a small odyssey.

The good news is that every year there are more **"playas caninas"** (dog beaches): stretches of coast designated by town halls so dogs can enjoy the sea with their owners. And with a campervan you have the perfect freedom to link them together into a road trip.

In this guide we explain how they work, which regions offer the most options, how to find the official up-to-date list and a suggested coastal route with all our tricks to keep the experience safe.

---

## 1. How Dog Beaches Work in Spain

A dog beach is not just "a beach where nobody says anything". It is a space that **the town hall officially designates**, usually by decree or bylaw, and it is normally signposted at the entrance.

What you need to know:

* **The designation changes every year.** Some towns renew their dog beach season after season, others move it to a different stretch and others drop it altogether. What applied last summer may not apply this year.
* **They usually have schedules and seasons.** Many operate only in summer, and some limit access to early morning and late afternoon.
* **They have their own rules.** The most common are:
  1. Carrying **your dog's paperwork** (microchip and up-to-date vaccinations) in case you are asked for it.
  2. **Always picking up poo** and leaving no litter.
  3. Keeping your dog **under control**: some require a lead at the access points or at all times.
  4. **Potentially dangerous dogs** muzzled and licensed, as required by the applicable rules.
  5. Sometimes, a maximum number of dogs per person.

> **Watch out:** outside the bathing season, many town halls allow dogs on beaches that are off-limits in summer, but **this is not a general rule**. Always check the signs at the entrance and the local bylaw.

## 2. Regions with the Most Options

These are the regions where, in our experience, it is easiest to find dog beaches. **It is not a closed or definitive list**: use it as a starting point.

| Region | What to expect | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Catalonia** | Several dog beaches along the coast of Barcelona, Girona and Tarragona. **Barcelona** has set aside a dog area on Llevant beach in recent seasons | Check the council calendar: it usually only operates in summer |

## 3. How to Find the Official, Up-to-Date List

Because designations change, **do not rely on old lists from blogs or forums** (this table included: treat it as a guide). The sources that do work are:

1. **The town hall website:** search for "playa canina" or, in Catalonia, "platja per a gossos" together with the town's name. That is where they usually publish the decree, the schedule and the rules.
2. **Local tourist offices:** a phone call or email saves you surprises.
3. **Regional and provincial tourism portals**, which sometimes compile all the dog beaches along their coast.
4. **The signs on site:** the beach signage always has the final say.

## 4. Suggested Coastal Van Route

This route runs down the Mediterranean from north to south, the coast with the most dog beaches. Allow **10–14 days** at a relaxed pace. Before each stage, check the town hall website to see which dog beaches are active that year.

### Stage 1: Costa Brava and Barcelona (2–3 days)
Start on the Girona coast, looking for quiet coves and towns with a dog area. Head down to **Barcelona** to try the dog area on Llevant beach if it is open that season.

### Stage 2: Costa Daurada and Terres de l'Ebre (2 days)
A stretch of wide, less crowded beaches. The long sandy beaches of southern Tarragona usually give you more room to find quiet spots.

### Stage 3: Valencian Community (3–4 days)
The heart of the route. From Castellón to Alicante you will find several official dog beaches. In **Valencia**, the Pinedo dog beach is a classic stop.

### Stage 4: Murcia and eastern Andalusia (3–4 days)
Finish in Almería or Málaga, combining dog beaches with trips into the inland mountains, which are cooler and have trails that are ideal for walking with a dog. Bear in mind that protected natural areas may restrict dogs: check before you go.

**Northern alternative:** if you are travelling in summer and your dog struggles with the heat, swap the route for **Galicia → Asturias → Cantabria → Basque Country**. Fewer dog beaches, but much more bearable temperatures.

## 5. Tips for a Beach Day with Your Dog

* **Heat and sun:** go early in the morning or at sunset. Bring a parasol or beach shelter and plenty of fresh water.
* **Salt water:** stop your dog drinking seawater; in large amounts it can cause vomiting and diarrhoea. Offer fresh water often.
* **Hot sand:** at midday it can burn their paw pads. Put your hand on the sand: if you cannot hold it there for five seconds, neither can they.
* **Rinse at the end:** shower them with fresh water to remove salt and sand, which irritate skin and eyes. Dry ears and skin folds well.
* **Sand hoppers ("sea fleas"):** these small jumping crustaceans at the water's edge can bother sensitive dogs. If you notice itching or redness, rinse and ask your vet.
* **Jellyfish:** pay attention to flags and warnings. If your dog gets stung, rinse with seawater (not fresh water), remove any remains without rubbing and see a vet if the reaction is strong.

## 6. Where to Sleep Nearby: Respect the Coastal Law

One of the most common mistakes is wanting to sleep right next to the dog beach. **Spain's Coastal Law (Ley de Costas) prohibits parking and spending the night in the maritime-terrestrial public domain**, and many coastal towns keep a particularly close eye on beach car parks in summer.

The best alternatives:

1. **Dog-friendly campsites** (ask in advance about supplements and restrictions).
2. **Motorhome aires**, municipal or private, a few kilometres from the coast.
3. **Nearby inland villages**, where parking up properly for the night is easier.

## 8. FAQ About Dog Beaches

### Can I take my dog to any beach out of season?
Not necessarily. Many town halls allow it outside the bathing season, but others keep the ban all year round. Check the local bylaw and the signs.

### Can I be fined for taking my dog to a non-designated beach?
Yes. Municipal bylaws set out fines, and the amount varies from town to town. Better not to risk it.

### Do I need to bring the vaccination record to a dog beach?
It is highly recommended. Some beaches explicitly require it and officers may ask to see it.
