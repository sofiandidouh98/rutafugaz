You have been on the road for three days, the fresh water tank is running on fumes, the grey tank is about to overflow and the chemical toilet is crying for help. This is the moment every van or motorhome traveller discovers the value of a good **motorhome service area** (*área de autocaravanas* in Spanish).

In just a few years Spain has gone from a handful of areas to **hundreds spread across the whole country**, many of them run by town councils and free of charge. But they do not all work the same way: some have electricity, others just a tap; some take coins, others tokens you buy at the village bar.

In this guide we explain exactly what they are, what services to expect, what they cost, which etiquette applies and how to empty your tanks like a veteran (without getting splashed or earning dirty looks).

---

## 1. Motorhome Area, Campsite or Car Park: What Is the Difference?

Although people often mix them up, they are three different things with different rules:

* **Motorhome area (*área AC*):** A space set up specifically for motorhomes and campervans, with a service point to fill up with water and empty tanks. It may have overnight bays or be service-only. As a rule, **you may only park there, not camp**.
* **Campsite:** A tourist business regulated by each Spanish region, with a reception, pitches, showers, toilets and often a pool or restaurant. Here **you can put out your awning, table and chairs**.
* **Authorised car park:** A car park where overnight stays are tolerated or allowed, but **with no services at all**. Fine for sleeping, useless for emptying tanks.

| Feature | Motorhome area | Campsite | Car park |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Drinking water refill** | ✅ Almost always | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| **Grey and black water disposal** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| **Electricity** | ⚠️ Sometimes | ✅ Usually | ❌ No |
| **Awning, table and chairs out** | ❌ Usually not | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |

| **Approximate price per night** | €0–15 | €20–45 | €0–10 |

*Approximate prices that vary a lot by area and season.*

## 2. Typical Services at an Area

The heart of any area is the **service point** (often called a *borne*, a word borrowed from French). These are the services it usually includes:

### Grey water disposal
Water from the sink and shower is emptied into a **grate or drain in the ground**. You park over it, open the drain valve and you are done. Some areas have a sloping platform so the tank drains completely.

### Black water disposal
The contents of the **chemical toilet cassette** go into a **dedicated hopper or inlet**, usually with a lid, at waist or ground level. There is normally a tap next to it for rinsing the cassette, which is **not for drinking**.

### Drinking water refill
A tap, often threaded, for filling your fresh water tank. **Carry your own food-grade hose** and a set of adapters, because taps are not always standard.

### Electricity
Not every area has it. When it does, it is usually via blue CEE sockets (the camping type) with **limited amperage** (often between 6 and 16 A) and frequently paid for by time.

## 3. Free or Paid: Approximate Price Ranges

Three models coexist in Spain:

1. **Free municipal areas:** Very common in inland villages that want to attract visitors. Services and overnight stays at no cost, in exchange for spending money in local shops.
2. **Paid municipal areas:** They charge per night, per service or both. Rough ranges: **€3–12 per night** and **€2–5 per service token**.
3. **Private areas:** Usually better equipped (electricity at every bay, security, sometimes a laundry or showers). Rough ranges: **€10–20 per night**, more on the coast in summer.

> **Watch out:** Rates change often and depend on the town and the season. Always check recent reviews in the apps before you go.

## 4. How Token and Coin Service Points Work

Many areas use **automatic service machines**, some from widespread European manufacturers such as Euro-Relais or Flot Bleu. Operation varies, but usually follows this pattern:

* **Coins:** You insert the amount and the machine releases water or electricity for a fixed time (for example, a few minutes of water or a few hours of power).
* **Tokens:** Tokens are sold at the town hall, tourist office, a bar or the nearby petrol station. The sign at the area usually says where. **Buy your token before you arrive** if the area is in a small village with short opening hours.
* **Card or app:** More and more modern areas let you pay by bank card or through a mobile app.

**Veteran tip:** Have the hose connected and the tank open *before* you insert the coin or token. The clock starts as soon as you pay and there is no extra time.

## 5. Maximum Stay and Etiquette

Most municipal areas limit how long you can stay, often to **24, 48 or 72 hours**. The limit is usually shown on the sign at the entrance and the idea is simple: keep the area rotating so more people can enjoy it.

Unwritten rules everyone expects you to follow:

* **Do not camp:** No awnings, tables, chairs or washing lines, unless the area explicitly allows them.
* **Do not hog the service point:** Use it, empty, fill and move away. Never park on it for the night.
* **Respect quiet hours** and avoid running generators at unsociable times.
* **Never rinse the cassette with the drinking water tap.** It is rule number one and the one most often broken.
* **Clean up after yourself:** If you splash, rinse the area with the service tap.
* **Spend money in the village:** Free areas exist because councils expect something back. Bread, coffee, the local market.

Remember that even in an area you are still subject to the general rules on overnight stays. If you want a refresher, read our [legal guide to camping vs overnight parking](/en/blog/camping-vs-overnight-parking-spain-legal-guide).

## 7. Checklist for Using the Disposal Point Properly

Save this list on your phone. It will spare you more than one mishap:

- [ ] **Put on disposable gloves** before touching anything.
- [ ] **Position the vehicle** with the grey water outlet right over the grate.
- [ ] **Empty the grey water first** and check that the tank is completely empty.
- [ ] **Take out the cassette** and carry it to the black water hopper, never to the grey water grate.
- [ ] **Empty it slowly**, opening the cassette's vent button to avoid splashing.
- [ ] **Rinse the cassette** with the service (non-drinking) tap.
- [ ] **Add the toilet chemical** and a little water to the cassette. Some areas ask for biodegradable products: check the sign.
- [ ] **Wash your hands** and change gloves before touching the drinking water hose.
- [ ] **Fill the fresh water tank** with your food-grade hose.
- [ ] **Check that everything is closed** and clean before you leave.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I stay overnight at any motorhome area?
Not always. Some are **service-only** and do not allow overnight stays. Others limit the number of bays or the length of stay. The sign at the entrance and the apps will tell you.

### Can small campervans use the areas?
Yes, in the vast majority. What matters is that you can use the service point without spilling anything. If your van has no grey tank, you can still use the area just to fill up with water.

### Are there areas open in winter?
Many are, although some cut off the water during frosty months to prevent burst pipes. If you are travelling in the low season, see our guide to [campsites open all year in Spain](/en/blog/campsites-open-all-year-spain-winter).
