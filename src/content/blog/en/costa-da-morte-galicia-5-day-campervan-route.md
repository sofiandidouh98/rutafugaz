The **Costa da Morte** ("Coast of Death") did not get its name by chance. For centuries, its cliffs, reefs and storms swallowed hundreds of ships, and even today the Atlantic pounds this corner of Galicia with impressive force.

But behind the name lie lonely lighthouses, wild and almost empty beaches, sanctuaries by the sea, a waterfall that drops straight into the ocean and some of the best goose barnacles in the world. For us, it is one of the most authentic van routes on the peninsula.

We suggest 5 days from north to south, from **Malpica to Carnota**, with approximate distances, where to sleep without hassle and everything you need to know about the rules on the Galician coast.

---

## Trip at a Glance
* **Length:** 5 days / 4 nights.
* **Total distance:** Approx. 200-250 km (from A Coruña to Muros).
* **Best time to go:** June to September. September is our favourite: fewer people and the sea still mild (for Galicia).
* **Driving difficulty:** Low-medium. Winding secondary roads and some narrow or unpaved tracks to lighthouses and beaches.
* **Key rule:** **The Coastal Law (Ley de Costas).** No overnight stays next to beaches, dunes or protected areas.

## 2. Day 1: Malpica and Laxe

Leave A Coruña in the morning and in just under an hour you will be in **Malpica de Bergantiños**, a real working fishing port with colourful boats and the **Sisargas Islands** just offshore. Climb up to the viewpoint at the hermitage of San Adrián for the best view.

On the way to Laxe, make a detour to the **Dolmen de Dombate** in Cabana de Bergantiños: one of the most important megalithic monuments in Galicia, protected under a modern roof. If you like goose barnacles, you are in **Roncudo** territory (Corme), whose *percebes* are famous all over Spain.

End the day in **Laxe**, with a long, quiet town beach, ideal for a sunset stroll.

---

## 3. Day 2: Camariñas and Cabo Vilán

**Camariñas** is famous for its **bobbin lace** (*palilleiras*): you will see women working at their front doors and shops selling handmade pieces.

From the village, follow the coast road to the **Cabo Vilán Lighthouse**, one of the most spectacular in Galicia, standing on a rocky crag with the sea crashing below. Further north is the **Cemiterio dos Ingleses** (English Cemetery), where the sailors of HMS Serpent, wrecked in 1890, were buried.

* **Watch the access:** part of the coastal track to the cemetery is narrow and may be unpaved. With a large camper, consider walking or cycling from a safe spot.

---

## 4. Day 3: Muxía and Fisterra

### Morning: Muxía
In **Muxía**, the **Sanctuary of Virxe da Barca** stands on the rocks, right at the water's edge. Next to it are the legendary stones (such as the *Pedra de Abalar*) and the sculpture **A Ferida** ("The Wound"), which commemorates the Prestige oil spill of 2002. Climb **Monte Corpiño** to see the whole headland from above.

### Afternoon: Fisterra
From Muxía it is about 30 km to **Fisterra**. Stroll around the harbour and save the best for last: **Cape Fisterra**, with its lighthouse and the **km 0.000 marker of the Camino de Santiago**. Watching the sun sink into the Atlantic from here is one of those moments that make the whole trip worthwhile.

* **Tip:** in summer the car park at the cape gets packed at sunset. Go up early or leave the van lower down and walk.

---

## 5. Day 4: Ézaro Waterfall and Carnota

The **Fervenza do Ézaro** is the mouth of the river Xallas, which falls straight into the sea as a waterfall. The flow depends largely on the hydroelectric plant, so it can vary a lot from one day to the next. If you want to drive up to the **Ézaro viewpoint**, bear in mind that the road is **extremely steep**: not suitable for every van.

A few kilometres away is **Carnota**, with one of the longest beaches in Galicia, about 7 km of white sand and dunes. Stick to the boardwalks: the dune system is protected. In the village, do not miss the **Carnota hórreo**, one of the largest granaries in Galicia.

---

## 6. Day 5: Monte Pindo and Muros

If you feel like a hike, **Monte Pindo** (the "Celtic Olympus") offers granite trails with views over the whole coast. Choose a signposted route and do not underestimate the heat or the fog.

Finish in **Muros**, a fishing town with arcades and a stone old quarter, already on the Muros e Noia estuary. From here, Santiago de Compostela is just over an hour away.

---

## 7. Where to Sleep and the Coastal Law

* **Municipal motorhome areas:** several local councils in the area have their own. They are the most convenient and legal option.
* **Campsites:** there are campsites all along the route, especially around Fisterra and Carnota. Book in August.
* **Coastal Law (Ley de Costas):** it prohibits parking and driving in the maritime-terrestrial public domain (beaches, dunes, cliffs) and restricts uses in the protection easement zone. On top of that, many councils have bylaws that **ban overnight stays near the coast**. Fines in Galicia are common in summer.
* **Protected areas:** part of this coastline is within the Natura 2000 network. No camping and no tables or chairs outside.

We sum it all up in our [legal guide to camping and overnight parking in Spain](/en/blog/camping-vs-overnight-parking-spain-legal-guide): **parked yes, camped no**, and always away from the sand.

## 8. Weather and Food

### The Weather
The Atlantic is in charge. Even in August there can be fog, strong winds and 18 °C days. Bring warm clothes, a rain jacket and levelling blocks for uneven ground. In stormy weather, **stay away from the rocks** at the capes: the waves here are treacherous.

### What to Eat
* **Percebes (goose barnacles):** the king of the Costa da Morte. The price varies a lot depending on the season and the sea.
* **Pulpo á feira** (Galician-style octopus) with boiled potatoes.
* **Empanada** (with scallops, tuna or sardines).
* **Spider crab, razor clams and fresh fish** straight from the fish markets in the ports.
* **Albariño or Ribeiro wine** and, to finish, a *queimada* if the occasion arises.

---

## 9. Practical Tips for Vans

1. **Water and waste disposal:** locate the areas with services before you set off; not all have them and some close out of season.
2. **Tracks to lighthouses:** many are narrow with no passing places. If in doubt, park and walk.
3. **Wind:** if you have a pop-up roof, do not raise it in strong wind, and park with the nose into the wind.
4. **Fuel:** there are petrol stations in the main towns (Carballo, Cee, Muros), but they are scarce on the more isolated stretches.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I sleep in my van on the beach in Galicia?
No. The Coastal Law and local bylaws prevent it on most beaches and coastal areas. Use motorhome areas, campsites or car parks in built-up areas where it is allowed.

### How many days do I need for the Costa da Morte?
With 5 days you can see the essentials at a relaxed pace. With 3 you can do Muxía, Fisterra, Ézaro and Carnota, but you will be rushing.

### When is the Ézaro waterfall at its best?
After rain and when the power plant releases more water. Ask at the Dumbría tourist office before you go.
